COMPARATIVE CULTURAL REPORT

Two names.
Two cultural stories.

We compare one English toponym and one Kazakhstani toponym to see how language encodes different relationships with place.

CHORONYM · INDUSTRIAL HERITAGE
KAZAKHSTAN

ORONYM · NATURAL HERITAGE
The Black Country and Bozzhyra are geographical names that reflect the history and identity of different cultures.

The Black Country is connected with the Industrial Revolution in England. Its name describes an industrial area whose nineteenth-century landscape was transformed by coal mining, iron foundries and factories. The name now represents industrial heritage, working communities and a strong regional identity.

Bozzhyra, on the other hand, is connected with the natural landscape of Kazakhstan. Its name is commonly analysed through Kazakh elements associated with a pale or grey colour and a ravine or gully. It evokes the distinctive terrain of Mangystau and can represent the country’s geological and natural heritage.

Both names have descriptive associations, but their cultural emphases differ. The Black Country recalls human industry and the transformation of a landscape, while Bozzhyra draws attention to the appearance and geological character of the land.

Both names are used literally as geographical names. They also carry wider cultural connotations: The Black Country can stand for industrial history and regional pride; Bozzhyra can stand for the distinctive natural heritage of Kazakhstan. A foreigner would need knowledge of the British Industrial Revolution and the local history of the West Midlands to understand the first name fully. To understand Bozzhyra, a foreigner would benefit from knowing the Kazakh language and the geography and cultural heritage of Mangystau.

THE COMPARATIVE INSIGHT

Toponyms preserve different kinds of collective memory. One can remember what people built; another can describe what the land looks like. Both become culturally meaningful when communities connect the name with shared knowledge and identity.
